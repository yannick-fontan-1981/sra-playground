## Joint routes and guards

`v6-coupled-pair-route.ts` transfers the complete six-field result of V6
`joint_route` and the V5 collision-search closure that this wrapper constructs.
The source requires measured equal vertical and opposite horizontal coupling,
at least two tested horizontal control references, an aligned measured lattice
and revealed floor patterns. Other coupling signs remain source refusals.

The search preserves canonical action order, tracked member identity,
independent collisions and the optional exclusion of unmatched floor mixtures.
It retains the original 40-action and 4096-expansion bounds. Visited states
and expanded states are separate counts. Parent links reconstruct the selected
path without copying every partial route into the search queue. Search data
is local to the call; no unbounded planning cache is introduced.

`v6-coupled-pair-planning.ts` uses the retained samples to construct the same
conditional proposal, evidence references, trial identity and expected member
boxes. The common adapter releases at most 16 actions. Its guard binds the
initial public frame, observation, epoch, score, command payload and current
member loci. Subsequent primitives retain the source geometry guards.
Unexpected movement or an extra effect seals the prefix and preserves the
refuted hypothesis and unresolved question.

## Auxiliary support and continuation

The three `v6-auxiliary-support*.ts` modules transfer
`AuxiliarySupportAccess`, `coupled_support_preparation` and
`support_continuation`. When the measured coupled route is unavailable, the
source can ask whether a distinct compact occurrence can be selected and
repositioned. Virtual erasure is only a planning question. The physical click,
mode-specific displacement, parking position and return to paired control each
require their own observed response.

Known control effects and a guarded route suffix can continue across committed
prefixes. An uncovered control relation is the final experimental primitive of
its prefix. Reconciliation then occurs once at the sealed boundary. Merely
restoring the pair's appearance does not certify restored control: the source
requires a physical paired-movement test while preserving the auxiliary.
Counterexamples and ambiguous control states are retained and can block
unrelated probing.

## One authoritative common memory

The existing `public_occurrence_recall` family remains the single owner of the
shared journal. `proposeInGroup` exposes coupled support in `goal_access` and
occurrence recall later in `window_constraints`, in source order. This avoids
duplicate acquisition and preserves the earlier recall priority over
SlotSubstitution where that entry applies.

At a sealed prefix, the supported acquisition order is recall, pair evidence,
auxiliary support evidence, then public packet observation. An auxiliary
group-control witness can emit a further pair event at its original journal
position. The family node and retained series fact carry the same actual state.
Reconstruction replays all supported event kinds and checks the complete native
closure digest before replacing any working memory. A rejected reconstruction
cannot partly clear one of the memories.

The digest scope is `native_recall_ancestors_and_coupled_support_v3`. It nests
the source pair and auxiliary layer formulas around the already transferred
recall closure. It is not the full original `ObservedCompositionConstraint`
inheritance digest. Remaining ancestors, including alternating-graph planning
and acquisition, have separate coverage. No ancestral event is silently
removed from the development oracle's raw evidence to claim global parity.

Planning copies only the current pair model, not the complete historical pair
journal. Existing V7 delta storage, shared immutable fragments, bounded caches,
revisions and lazy inspection are preserved. This does not establish bounded
memory for arbitrarily long source journals.

### Native checkpoint correction

The first real campaign exposed growing costs even for single-transition
acquisitions. Besides the expected cost of acquiring several actions at a
sealed boundary, the runtime repeatedly copied and replayed the same shared
journal during fact retention, family checkpointing and the next plan.

The opt-in `nativeState` / `restoreNative` path now uses the existing
`nativePut` / `nativeGet` ownership contract and the store's immutable
hydration. Previously validated events are shared between checkpoints. The
same immutable state can be reused only while the working memories and journal
still match their mutation certificate. Public snapshots remain independent;
new values, rollbacks, mutable inputs, changed fragments and direct working
container edits still require complete reconstruction and digest verification.
Acquisition invalidates certification before it can make partial changes.

The source digest formulas are unchanged. No counterexample, historical event
or V7 revision is discarded. The twelve deterministic checkpoint tests count
actual reducer calls and verify rollback, corrupted state, shallow freezing,
public mutations and a changed fragment under an unchanged root. A further
test exercises the actual common runtime, including the retained source fact,
through the new hooks. Computing a new source digest and recording cumulative
evidence still depends on history; this change removes redundant work rather
than promising constant-cost long sessions.

## Dense-scene refusal and explicit V7 continuation

The initial DICO campaign on `0.6.1` completed four levels in 31 actions.
Its 32nd physical action could not be acquired because the next public scene
exceeded the common mechanical learner's 512-entity domain. The failed
[campaign](native-campaigns-v35/dico-before-domain-handling.json) is retained.

A [witness-only diagnostic](native-campaigns-v35/dico-domain-witness-diagnostic.json)
reproduced all 33 public packets and compared the complete final two neutral
observations to the unchanged Python observer. Both implementations measure
530 and 531 entities. The source learner rejects those same observations, and
the source common caller logs and rethrows this error. The isolated
SubsetDiagnosis six-level oracle never invokes this common mechanical learner;
its success was not proof of complete-caller success.

Version `0.6.2` therefore makes an explicit V7 robustness extension, rather
than claiming that an exception handler was omitted from the transfer.
`v6-native-transition-domain.ts` converts only the typed neutral entity-domain
refusal into an `unknown` assessment. The source 512 limit and pure functions'
refusal remain unchanged. Other errors propagate, and all in-domain assessments
retain the original results. A [source boundary audit](native-campaigns-v35/neutral-domain-original.json)
records twelve unchanged Python calls at 512 and 513 entities.

An unavailable mechanical assessment cannot validate or refute an old rule,
construct a new one, update either registry, or release an action. The common
Frontier still records the observed public transition, with zero new mechanical
rules. Other independent source families may continue through the usual
selection and WHY/ACT gate. The transition's actual V7 node retains both full
neutral observations and both complete public animation packets; immutable
fragments share unchanged data. No entity is removed or merged to fit the limit.
The extension is declared in runtime coverage and in each affected assessment.
It is not counted as an additional transferred source entry or as exact caller
parity outside the original domain.

## M0R0 campaign and the fourth-level limit

The first campaign, on the recorded `0.6.0` source before native checkpoint
reuse, reached **3/6 levels in one session**. The first three completed levels
used 19, 27 and 62 actions. The run then stopped at the normal 360-second active
budget of level 4, with 151 total actions. This is a failed six-level campaign,
not a complete-game win. Its [original report](native-campaigns-v35/m0r0-before-native-checkpoint.json)
is retained unchanged.

A [witness-only replay](native-campaigns-v35/m0r0-witness-diagnostic.json)
reproduced all 152 public packets for those 151 commands, including animations.
No new agent decisions or private game-state reads were used. The level-4
pair, all four movements, both coupling signs and the compact auxiliary were
measured successfully. Its 43 actions consisted of four directional responses
and 39 later clicks that did not move the pair.

The [route-question diagnostic](native-campaigns-v35/m0r0-route-question-diagnostic.json)
then examined only the saved public image. With the source precaution enabled,
the real scene exhausts its search in 37 states; virtually erasing the single
recognized auxiliary still exhausts it in 186 states. Eighteen reached cells
mix the revealed floor palette with another palette, and their contact is
excluded by the original policy. This is not the 4096-expansion or 40-action
bound. A relaxed counterfactual explains the restriction but was never executed
in the game. The production precaution remains unchanged; faster checkpointing
does not itself supply the missing causal question.

campaign in one session: **108 actions, 34 sealed prefixes, and 19/27/62

[comparison script](native-campaigns-v35/compare-m0r0-native-checkpoint.mjs)

The directly comparable first-two-level segment is 0.129 seconds slower
(0.26%) in this run, so it demonstrates **no net timing improvement**. The
three-level total is lower, but the endpoint differs: the old target of six
levels performs a third natural boundary replay into level 4; the new target
of three levels stops after acquiring level 3, with two boundary replays.
That omission prevents attributing the level-3 or total reduction to
checkpoint reuse. These are integrated real-active-time observations, not
an isolated CPU benchmark or a count of eliminated copies/restores. No full
graph equality or complete M0R0 win is claimed; the revalidation proves that
the successful three-level public trajectory is preserved.
