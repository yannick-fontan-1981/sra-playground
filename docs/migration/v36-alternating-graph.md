## Measurement and conditional topology

The measurement uses the original four-connected components and the consumed crop-sprite projection: sprite identity, bounding box, pixel pattern, palette histogram, occurrence count and frame-boundary flag. It preserves component order, excludes the same frame-enclosing components, counts occurrences across provisional crops and caches eight descriptions by actual raster content. Returned proposals and helper results remain separate from that immutable cache.

Membership references, internal component counts, exact dezoom factors, periodic-grid results, holes and other unconsumed parts of `describe_visual_scene` are outside this transfer. The source module remains present in the immutable reference and partial in the coverage map.

Alternating graph candidates require repeated full squares on a unique lattice phase, compatible connector squares and exceptional same-footprint overlays. Their material values remain descriptive. An identified overlay and measured action-conditioned translation are required before a route becomes available to the planner. An untested color, marker or uniform square is not silently promoted to a control, hazard or proven terminal role.

The measurement preserves the original limits of 512 expanded states, route depth 40, three retained route witnesses and three accepted description slots. A fourth description triggers the original ambiguity/bound result. The multicolored-occupant precaution permits at most three additional occupants and requires the original cardinal singleton geometry. Its exclusion of a node in front of a marker is a counterfactual hypothesis supplied by the DRM policy.

The topology helper uses equivalent sets and maps, represented with row/column keys in TypeScript. Instrumental contact searches a position and removal-mask state under the original 512-examination bound and remaining-action limit. A `bigint` mask preserves Python's arbitrary-width integer behavior. Parent links reconstruct the route without copying every historical prefix into the search queue.

Removing an occupant in a planning state requires retained prior removal evidence with matching histogram and size. The physically released prefix ends at the first new contact. A counterfactual complete route is evidence for the question being asked; it is not an authorization to execute its entire imagined consequence.

## Access, learning and primitive guards

The access layer retains the original seven fields: samples, refused trials, terminal observations, emitted events, calibration, contacts and removals. Stable effectful translations are acquired from uniquely matched public overlays. Conflicting or insufficient controls preserve the original abstention or unresolved blocking result.

Admission binds the epoch, score, command sequence, availability and current actor histogram. The first primitive additionally binds the original public-frame digest and observation reference. Subsequent outcome checks compare the expected displacement, the revealed support and effects outside the permitted departure/destination areas. An official boundary or terminal observation seals the prefix without converting unknown visual causality into a rule.

A nonordinary contact remains unknown. Merely covering another overlay does not establish its removal: the original acquisition requires a later departure that reveals ordinary support. Failed predictions, refused trials and ambiguous contact evidence stay in retained state.

The access oracle preserves the original raw ancestor returns. It additionally identifies the graph-owned event suffix and compares the graph layer's fields and digest. Its digest test nests those fields around the actual source parent digest; it does not replace the ancestor with a fabricated empty state or claim that every inherited capability has been migrated.

In this access oracle's controlled journals, the actual `CellPlacementAccess.record` parent-event returns are empty and are preserved as empty returns. These cases therefore do not verify a nonempty acquisition by that inherited layer; the broader inheritance remains partial.

Unsealed responses have level medians of 20.9, 20.4 and 20.4 ms, with an overall range of 17.9–27.2 ms. No increasing latency appears in that phase in this run. Sealing includes unequal workloads: one 16-action prefix takes 4.216 s to acquire. The three later one-action graph prefixes still take 1.408, 1.473 and 1.571 s. Their scenes, contact states and retained history differ, so the differences do not isolate history accumulation as a cause. They do show remaining pauses at acquisition.

The [CPU summary](native-campaigns-v36/tu93-cpu-summary.json) contains 68.169 sampled seconds, including process startup, game hosting and validation. Explicit helper inspection accounts for 14.268 s (20.9%); it is outside the request timings above. The largest sampled leaf modules are `canonical.ts` at 26.579 s and `bounded-weak-cache.ts` at 16.799 s. These overlap the inspection category and must not be added to it. Outside that explicit inspection category, their self samples remain 20.262 s and 10.032 s respectively. Visible callers include native checkpoint, commit, retained-digest and restore paths; async stack loss limits exact attribution.

The next performance priority is therefore repeated canonical validation/fingerprinting and bounded-cache insertion/eviction during sealed-prefix acquisition and checkpointing. Preserve the ownership checks, exact source digests, bounded cache policy and retained revisions, then compare the same retained public trajectory before claiming an improvement. Extending TU93 beyond three levels remains a separate resolution test.

The [raw profile archive](native-campaigns-v36/tu93.cpuprofile.gz) is 711,495 bytes compressed; the request audit records both archive and uncompressed SHA256 hashes. No further game or performance run was used for this analysis.
